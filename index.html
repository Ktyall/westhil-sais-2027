01 / Process

What the self-study actually is.

With the shorter runway, the five-part cycle splits into two clear phases. The first two steps — read the indicators, inventory the evidence — are due October 7 and are the whole job right now. Rating, narrative, and the improvement plan happen after the pre-visit, once every committee's evidence is in hand.

NOW
i.

Read the indicators

Each standard has 4–7 indicators. Read all of yours against the 2022 self-study first — most haven't changed. The indicators define what "meeting the standard" looks like in SAIS's language.

DUE OCT 7
ii.

Inventory evidence

List what already exists — policies, minutes, surveys, programs, data. This is the only deliverable that matters before October 7. If an indicator has no evidence, flag the gap; don't stop to solve it yet.

LATER
iii.

Rate honestly

For each indicator: Not Yet · Developing · Meeting · Exceeding. Inflated ratings are the fastest way to lose credibility with the visiting team.

LATER
iv.

Write the narrative

A short response per indicator. Cite the evidence by name. Note what's strong, what's emerging, what's planned. Two paragraphs is enough — clarity beats volume.

JAN
v.

Improvement plan

Identify one to two real priorities per standard. These become the school's commitments for the next cycle — and what the visiting team will ask about most.

04 / Expectations

What committee work looks like in practice.

Two columns. The left is the temptation; the right is the discipline. With the schedule compressed, the only thing that matters right now is staying on the right for evidence — narrative discipline comes back into focus after Nov 4.

Do
  • Start from the 2022 archive. Pull the equivalent indicator's evidence list first and check what's still current — don't build from a blank page.
  • Name the actual document, policy, or program, and where it lives. "Faculty Handbook §4.2, Drive folder," not "the handbook somewhere."
  • Log evidence as you find it, in the shared inventory — don't wait to compile it all at once the week of Oct 7.
  • Flag gaps immediately instead of quietly searching for weeks. A named gap becomes a plan; a hidden one becomes a scramble in November.
  • Meet every Friday between now and October 7. Minutes filed in your committee folder within 48 hours, using the template.
  • Treat October 7 as final. Whatever isn't inventoried by then has to be found in the Oct 8 – Nov 4 buffer, which is thin.
Don't
  • Start drafting indicator narratives yet. Ratings and write-ups come after the pre-visit — evidence first, full stop.
  • Cut and paste 2022 evidence citations without confirming the document still exists and is current.
  • Assume "someone else has it." If an indicator's evidence isn't logged by your committee, it doesn't count as inventoried.
  • Wait for the weekly meeting to report a gap. Message your lead the moment you find one.
  • Let one person carry an indicator alone if they're behind. Redistribute inside the committee before October 7, not after.
  • Treat Nov 4 as flexible. It's the pre-visit — evidence has to be genuinely ready, not "almost."
05 / Playbook

Run your meetings like the report depends on them.

Below: the agenda for the evidence-sprint reset, the weekly Friday rhythm through October 7, the note-taking protocol, and the indicator response template. Each committee follows the same playbook so the final report has one voice, not five.

PLAYBOOK · A

Sprint reset
Aug 21 · 60 min

All committees concurrent · schedule allowing

Purpose

The timeline changed — the visit moved up and evidence is now due October 7, not next spring. This meeting resets each committee around one job only: what evidence do we still need, who's finding it, and by when before Oct 7?

Agenda 60 MIN

0:00 – 0:05
New timeline, plain language. Lead states the three dates: evidence due Oct 7, pre-visit Nov 4 (all evidence must be ready), report due Feb 16, visit Mar 16–18.
0:05 – 0:20
Pull the 2022 file. Open the committee's 2022 evidence folder side-by-side with this year's indicators. For each one: does this document still exist and still apply?
0:20 – 0:45
Reassign by gap, not by indicator. Skip full re-reads. Focus only on indicators with thin or missing evidence — assign an owner and a find-by date inside the sprint window.
0:45 – 0:55
Confirm the Friday slot. Same time, every Friday, through October 2. Add a working session the week of Oct 5 if the inventory isn't closed.
0:55 – 1:00
Minutes & close. Confirm note-taker. File minutes in committee folder within 48 hours.

What success looks like

  • Every member knows the Oct 7 evidence deadline and the Nov 4 pre-visit gate.
  • Every indicator has an owner for its evidence, even if the indicator itself was already assigned in the spring.
  • The 2022 evidence list has been checked, not re-created — only genuine gaps get new work.
  • The weekly meeting time is confirmed through October 2.
  • Minutes are filed within 48 hours.

PLAYBOOK · C

Note-taking
The minute protocol

Filed within 48h · template in Drive

Why minutes matter

The visiting team can ask to see committee minutes. More importantly: SAIS expects evidence that the self-study was a genuine process, not a document produced in February. Your minutes are that evidence.

Required fields

  • Date, attendees, absent. Plain log.
  • Indicators discussed. By number (e.g. "3.2, 3.4"). Not "we talked about teaching."
  • Decisions made. Each decision in one sentence. "Rated 2.3 as Meeting based on board minutes from Oct 2024 and audit letter."
  • Evidence cited or requested. What documents came up, what's still needed.
  • Disagreements. Yes — write them down. Visitors trust committees that argued.
  • Action items. Owner · deliverable · due date. Three columns, no prose.

How to write a decision line

Weak: "We discussed indicator 2.5 and felt it was strong."

Strong: "Rated indicator 2.5 as Meeting. Evidence: Board minutes Sept 2024 (head's evaluation), HR file (succession plan draft), strategic plan §3. Gap: no documented board self-evaluation since 2023 — flagged for improvement plan."

PLAYBOOK · D

Indicator response
The three-part template

SAIS-mandated structure · every indicator

SAIS requires three parts per indicator

This is not a Westhill convention — it's the structure SAIS expects in every response: Affirm · Describe · Evidence. Plus a rating. Between now and October 7, only fill in the Evidence field. Affirm, Describe, and the rating wait until after the Nov 4 pre-visit — don't spend sprint time on prose that isn't due yet.

Indicator [Paste the full indicator text verbatim from the SAIS standards document] Rating Not Yet · Developing · Meeting · Exceeding 1. Affirmation One sentence: yes, the school adheres to this indicator. (Or: the school does not currently adhere — only if accurate, and only if a plan follows.) 2. Description — how we adhere 2–4 sentences. The actual practice, program, structure, or policy that satisfies the indicator. Name names. "The Academic Council, chaired by the Director of Academics, meets monthly to review curriculum mapping…" 3. Evidence A bullet list. Document name · location · date. "Academic Council minutes (Drive · 2025–26 folder · Sept 2025 onward)" Strengths · Emerging · Improvement note Optional but recommended. What's working, what's developing, and anything this committee thinks should become an improvement priority.

Length target

120 – 200 words per indicator. Shorter than you'd think. Visitors read fifty of these — clarity beats volume every time.

06 / Example

One indicator, two responses.

A worked comparison, drawn from a real Standard 3 (Teaching & Learning) indicator. Same prompt, two responses. Bookmark this — it's the standard to write to once drafting opens after November 4, not before.

Weak response · 47 words

"The school provides high-quality, sustained professional learning for all faculty, aligned to school priorities."

Westhill is deeply committed to professional development. Our faculty participate in numerous PD opportunities throughout the year, and we offer ongoing training that aligns with our institutional values. Teachers consistently report a positive experience.

Strong response · 168 words

"The school provides high-quality, sustained professional learning for all faculty, aligned to school priorities."

Meeting · trending toward Exceeding. Westhill's PD program shifted in 2023 from generic whole-faculty sessions to a menu-based model, after the 2022 self-study flagged that PD was perceived as "burdensome" and "not dynamic."

Faculty now select from targeted strands (IB pedagogy, assessment design, EAL strategies, digital tools) aligned to the three-year strategic plan. Evidence: PD Calendar 2025–26, Strand Enrollment Sheet, Faculty PD Survey (April 2026).

The survey shows participation up from 61% to 89% and "meaningfulness" ratings up from 2.8 to 4.1 on a 5-point scale. The Master's program for English speakers continues as a sustained, multi-year learning pathway.

Emerging: follow-through after individual sessions remains uneven — a 2022 weakness that has narrowed but not closed. The committee proposes a peer-coaching layer in 2026–27 as the standard's improvement priority.
